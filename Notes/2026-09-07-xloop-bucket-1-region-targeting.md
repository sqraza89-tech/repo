# xLoop — Bucket 1 region targeting

Research prepared for the meeting with the Head of Sales. Deliverable:
`2026-09-07-xloop-bucket-1-region-targeting.docx` (same folder).

## Brief as received

> "1) region division based on two factors
> Bucket 1) Economies that have a talent problem & are thriving
> Strict employment laws / legal considerations, tough hiring & firing laws"

Bucket 2 was not received. Scope confirmed with Sana: **Bucket 1 only**, applied to

## The finding that changes the brief

The three tests conflict. In Europe the worst tech talent shortages sit in the
slowest-growing economies, and the fastest-growing economies have the loosest labour
markets.

- Germany — 72.4% cannot fill ICT vacancies (worst in EU), GDP growth 0.8%
- Poland — 32.3% cannot fill (near-lowest), GDP growth 3.3% (fastest in EU)
- Spain — 30.1% cannot fill (lowest in EU), GDP growth 2.1%

**Recommendation: replace "thriving economy" with "growing IT budget."** European IT
spending is forecast +11.1% in 2026 against ~1% GDP growth. Under that definition the
bucket becomes internally consistent.

## Measure used

`Unmet ICT hiring need = (% enterprises recruiting ICT specialists) × (% of those that
could not fill)` — the share of all enterprises carrying an unfilled tech hiring need.
Multiplied by the count of enterprises with 50+ employees it gives addressable accounts.

| Market | Unmet need | Addressable accts | OECD EPRC | GDP 26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | 8.7% | ~6,980 | 2.33 | 0.8% |
| Netherlands | 9.7% | ~1,330 | 2.88 | 1.2% |
| Belgium | 9.1% | ~660 | 2.71 | 0.7% |
| Denmark | 8.0% | ~460 | 1.94 | 2.0% |
| Sweden | 5.4% | ~447 | 2.54 | 2.0% |

## Ranking

- **Tier 1** — Netherlands (cleanest fit), Germany (volume prize), Belgium (strongest legal push)
- **Tier 2** — UAE / Saudi Arabia, Sweden & Denmark, Austria
- **Tier 3** — France, Ireland, Japan, Italy
- **Excluded** — US & UK (fail the law test: EPRC 1.31 and 1.90); Poland, Czechia,
  Romania, Portugal (supply markets, not buyers); Spain (no shortage to sell against);
  India (supply market)

## The part that matters most for sales

In every Tier 1 market the law that creates the demand also makes **staff augmentation
the wrong product**. Germany's AÜG (18-month cap, Scheinselbständigkeit audits), the
Dutch Wet DBA (penalties resume 1 Jan 2026), Belgium's Article 31 of the Act of
24 July 1987 (making personnel available is *prohibited*), and France's *prêt de
main-d'œuvre illicite* / *marchandage* (absence of an *obligation de résultat* is an
indicator of illegality) all penalise client-directed supplied labour.

Fixed-scope, deliverable-based managed delivery with an xLoop-side lead sits outside all
four regimes — and is the reason those buyers pay a premium.

Language changes implied:
- retire "staff augmentation" → "specialist engineering capacity" / "managed delivery pod"
- lead with scope, not seats
- name the xLoop-side lead in every proposal

## GCC exception

UAE and Saudi fail the literal EPL test but quota law does the same job: Emiratisation
(10% of skilled roles by 31 Dec 2026, AED 9,000/month per unfilled slot ≈ AED 108k/year)
and Saudization (25–35% in tech; yellow/red band = no expat tech visas). Each extra local
hire raises the quota obligation; contracted offshore delivery does not. Fastest place to
test the whole thesis because xLoop already has Dubai and Doha HQs and Saudi delivery
evidence.

## Constraints flagged

- **No named European reference.** Marketing Brain: no offering has a grade-A proof
  point. This, not market choice, is the likeliest reason Tier 1 stalls.
- **Data transfer.** Pakistan has no EU adequacy decision; SCCs + TIA needed. Contracting
  via Dubai/Doha does not fix it — UAE and Qatar are not adequate either.
- **OECD EPL data is 2019** (latest published edition). Contracting-law points in §6 are
  all current and checked individually.
- Nothing here measures conversion — no CRM, no win/loss record.
