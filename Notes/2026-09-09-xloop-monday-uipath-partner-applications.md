## UiPath — blocked at step 1, and the routing problem

Landing page `https://www.uipath.com/partners/become-a-partner/business` is marketing only. The real
application is behind **Apply now** →
`https://partnerportal.uipath.com/s/?language=en_US&showForm=true`.

**It is a two-step gated form.** Step 1 asks only for business email + country; the remaining field
set does not load until that is submitted and the email validated. Not submitted — the step-2
fields remain unknown and need mapping once Huzaifa opens it.

Two findings worth keeping:

- Pakistan, UAE, Qatar and Saudi Arabia are all in the country list — no route is closed.
- The form states that **in certain geographies UiPath engages through an Authorized Distributor**
  who receives the applicant's business information. Pakistan is very likely one of those, meaning
  a Pakistan application routes to a regional distributor and xLoop onboards second-tier under that
  distributor's terms. **This is the one case where applying from the Dubai IFZA entity may be
  commercially better** — UAE coverage is likely more direct.
- Correct track is **Services / Solution Provider (implementation)**, not reseller, since licences
  flow through the distributor anyway. Expect to be asked about certified UiPath developers on
  staff — xLoop has none today, and UiPath Academy certifications are free.

### The framing decision for this application

*RPA Practice*, *Customer Landscape* and *Technical Resources* all assess an existing RPA business —
certified developers, RPA revenue, automation customers. **xLoop's honest answers there are close to
zero.** Two possible framings:

1. "We are an RPA firm" — the form's default assumption. Fails on evidence.
2. **"We are an enterprise AI and data engineering firm building an automation practice, and
   UiPath's agentic pivot is why now."** ← **chosen.** True, aligned with the Marketing Brain
   positioning, and matches who UiPath is actively recruiting (AI-capable partners, not more classic
   RPA shops).

Framing 2 must be consistent across **every** free-text box in the form.

## Strategic position given to Sana

**These are not three versions of the same bet.**

- **UiPath is the only one that fits xLoop's actual positioning.** Enterprise automation sold to
  banks, government, energy and manufacturing — the Priority 1–3 industries and the existing
  CIO/CTO/CISO buyer. Maps onto the *Make AI work / Make AI possible* pillars, and UiPath's
  agentic-AI pivot sits on top of xLoop's story. Push this hardest, and start now because it is the
  only one needing upfront investment (certifications).
- **Pipedrive and monday.com are the same bet twice** — same SMB/mid-market buyer, same low-ticket
  motion, and monday.com CRM competes head-on with Pipedrive. Neither vendor disqualifies you for
  the other; the risk is internal: first-year seat commitments to two competing products with no
  CRM resale history and nobody dedicated to either.
- **If picking one of the two: monday.com.** Bigger TAM, better partner economics, and decisively —
  it is not a CRM, so it does not collide with clients who already have one. Work-management is a
  wedge into almost any mid-market account, and the app marketplace gives xLab a second revenue
  line Pipedrive has no equivalent for.
- **Submit both, resource one.** Applications are cheap and the qualification calls are free market
  research — but the resourcing choice should be deliberate, not drift.

**Promotion position unchanged:** nothing on website or socials until something is signed, and then
a partner-directory listing plus a logo strip, not a campaign. Three partner badges announced at
once reads as a firm broadening out — the opposite of what the Marketing Brain says xLoop needs.
