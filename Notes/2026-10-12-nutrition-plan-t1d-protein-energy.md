# Nutrition plan — protein, energy and strength (Type 1 diabetes)

General nutrition guidance, not medical advice. Take this to my endocrinologist or a dietitian
before changing anything, and **never change insulin doses from this note.**

## My numbers
- 54.4 kg · 5 ft 1.5 in (156 cm) · BMI about 22 (normal)
- Type 1 diabetes: NovoRapid before meals, Toujeo 24 units at bedtime
- Rovista (rosuvastatin) for borderline-high cholesterol
- Creatine, half a teaspoon daily, started 2026-10-11
- Goals: more energy, more strength, a flatter tummy
- Teaching 4–5 aerial classes a week from Nov 2026 (see [[2026-10-08-aerial-yoga-instructor-setup]])

## Targets
- **Protein: 75–85 g a day** (about 1.4–1.6 g per kg). Spread over the day: 20–30 g at each meal
- **Calories: roughly 1,700–1,800.** No big deficit. At a normal BMI the aim is to swap fat for muscle, not to lose weight. Under-eating on insulin means lows and tiredness
- **Carbs: moderate and steady**, about 30–40 g per main meal, mostly slow carbs (roti, daal, oats, fruit). Steady carbs make NovoRapid dosing easier
- **Fats: cholesterol-friendly.** Olive or canola oil, nuts, fish. Less ghee, fried food and bakery items
- **Fiber:** vegetables or salad at lunch and dinner; daal or chana daily
- **Water:** 2–2.5 litres, more on class days (creatine needs it)

### Easy swaps
- **Breakfast:** oats cooked in milk + 1 scoop whey stirred in after cooking · besan chilla with yogurt · paneer bhurji with 1 roti
- **Lunch / dinner:** chana or lobia salad with a boiled egg · grilled fish with sautéed vegetables · chicken tikka with raita and salad · daal + egg curry
- **Snacks:** boiled eggs · roasted chana · paneer cubes · yogurt with chia seeds · peanut butter on a slice of multigrain bread
- **Twice a week:** fish (good for cholesterol)

## Supplements

### Whey protein — yes, on days food falls short
- 1 scoop (usually 20–24 g protein) in water. Best after a class or my own training, or as the afternoon snack
- Check the label for carbs. Mixed with milk it adds about 12 g of carbs per cup
- A big protein hit can raise glucose a few hours later. Watch my readings the first few times

### Creatine — fine to continue, with two checks
- It is not an energy booster like caffeine. It helps muscles with short, hard efforts (pull-ups, tucks) and the effect builds over 3–4 weeks
- Usual dose is 3 g a day. Half a teaspoon is about that. No need for more
- **Tell my doctor I've started.** Creatine raises the creatinine number on a kidney blood test without harming the kidneys, so the doctor needs to know when reading my results. With Type 1, get kidney function checked (creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin) if it hasn't been done this year
- I'm on a statin: if I get unusual muscle pain, weakness or dark urine, stop and call the doctor

### Glucerna — not needed daily
- It's a meal replacement made for people who struggle to eat enough. A serving has roughly 10 g protein and 25 g or more of carbs (check the label), so it needs insulin like a small meal
- Whey + real food gives more protein with fewer carbs
- Useful only as a **backup**: a day with no time to eat, or when I'm unwell. Keep one or two in the bag, don't make it a habit

## Teaching days — blood sugar safety
Hanging upside down while low is dangerous for me and for the class.
- **Check glucose before every class.** Common guidance is to start exercise around 126–180 mg/dL (7–10 mmol/L). Below about 90 mg/dL (5 mmol/L), eat 15–20 g of fast carbs first and wait
- **Hypo kit on the mat, every class:** glucose tablets or a small juice. Tell one person at each studio where it is
- **Never demo an inversion if I'm low or dropping fast.** Cue from the floor instead
- **Eat a proper meal 2–3 hours before class;** protein snack after
- **After evening classes, check before bed.** Exercise can cause lows hours later, overnight
- **Ask the doctor:** should NovoRapid for the meal before class, or Toujeo on teaching days, be adjusted now that I'm exercising 4–5 times a week? Don't adjust on my own

## Why I might be low on energy — things to check
- Blood sugar swinging high and low (both cause tiredness)
- Not eating enough for the amount I'm moving
- Sleep, and doing too much at once
- Blood tests worth asking for: HbA1c, **thyroid (TSH)**, **vitamin B12**, **vitamin D**, **iron/ferritin**, kidney function, lipids. Thyroid problems and B12 deficiency are more common with Type 1

## The tummy
- Fat can't be lost from one spot. What works: enough protein + strength work 2–3 times a week + sleep
- Teaching and drilling in the hammock is already core training. Add 2 short strength sessions (squats, rows, push-ups, bridges, hollow holds)
- Fewer lows means fewer "rescue" snacks, which helps more than cutting meals
- Give it 8–12 weeks. Measure the waist and take a photo monthly instead of watching the scale (creatine can add 0.5–1 kg of water in the muscles)
