## Post 4: Irfan Shaikh, Senior Solution Architect

**Tue 24 Nov 2026 · Irfan Shaikh · ICP2 · Text**

> Before switching on an AI agent inside a CRM, these are the five things I check first.
>
> 1. **Is the data clean enough?** An agent working from duplicate or outdated customer records makes confident mistakes. Fix the data first.
> 2. **What can it see, and what can it change?** Give it the same permissions you'd give a new hire on day one, not admin rights.
> 3. **Where does a person approve?** Decide which steps the agent can finish on its own and which need a human sign-off. Write it down.
> 4. **Can you trace every action?** If an auditor asks why a request was approved, you need the log, the data used and who signed off.
> 5. **Start with one workflow.** Pick something high volume and low risk, like routing service requests. Prove it, then expand.
>
> Platforms like Creatio make the agent part easier. These five decisions are still yours.
>
> Which of these does your team find hardest?
>
> #AIAgents #CRM #DataQuality #Creatio

## Post 6: Muhammad Humza, ISO 42001/27001 Lead Auditor

**Tue 8 Dec 2026 · Muhammad Humza · ICP1 · Text**

> If your bank puts AI agents into its CRM, here's what I'd expect an auditor to ask.
>
> 1. Which decisions can the agent make on its own, and who approved that list?
> 2. What customer data does it use, and is that allowed under your data rules?
> 3. Can you show the log for any single action it took?
> 4. Who owns the risk if it gets something wrong?
> 5. How do you switch it off, and who can do that?
>
> None of these are reasons to slow down. They're the questions to answer at the design stage, when it's cheap, rather than after go-live.
>
> ISO 42001 gives a clear structure for this. It's the standard for managing AI, and it works well alongside platforms like Creatio that keep a person in the loop.
>
> Which of these would your team struggle to answer today?
