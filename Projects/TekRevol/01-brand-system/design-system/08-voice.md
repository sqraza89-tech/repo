# 8. Voice

Proposed, based on TekRevol's Messaging and Positioning (AI-First) doc. It needs sign-off from someone with brand authority.

## The sound

Clear, direct and specific. It should sound like a sharp in-house engineer explaining the plan, not an agency describing itself. Confident without showing off.

A working example of the target voice, already on the site: "you want architectural truth, not a pitch."

## Rules

- No em dashes, anywhere
- Active voice. Short sentences of varied length
- Speak to one buyer's real situation at a time. Never "from startups to enterprises"
- Every claim traces to something real: a sourced number, a credential, a named case study
- No invented statistics. Claims about faster or cheaper AI-assisted delivery stay out until a measured engagement backs them
- Name the situation, never the vendor. Don't name rival agencies, platforms or AI tools in public copy
- Avoid stacked rule-of-three phrasing, "whether X, Y, or Z" constructions and false "from X to Y" ranges
- Define any jargon the first time it appears

## Headline directions by audience

| Audience | Leads with | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Founders | Trust | "Built by people who finish what the last team, or the last AI tool, started." |
| SMB owners | Speed | "Your business is already running. Your software should be too." |
| Mid-market champions | Something to take upstairs | "A proposal your leadership will actually approve." |
| Mid-market approvers | Compliance | "Built to the standard your industry already requires." |

## How the brand idea sounds in copy

Show growth through specifics, not slogans. Good:

- "Every build teaches us something. The next one starts from there."
- "We moved to AI-native delivery early. Your project gets what we learned."

Avoid: "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "next-gen", "unleash", "supercharge", "seamless", "harness the power of AI".
