# Safety — my non-negotiables

Sana is not certified. This file is built from widely used aerial yoga studio practice, not a
training manual. It is the safety source for `aerial-lesson-plan` until a certified source
replaces it. When in doubt, use the easier option.

## Set up before the first class (Nov 2026)
- [ ] First aid + CPR course (a one-day course is enough to start)
- [ ] Ask each studio: does their insurance cover me as the instructor? Do they have a waiver / health form? If not, make one
- [ ] Get Komal to walk me through each studio's rigging: who checks it, how often, and what to do if something looks wrong
- [ ] Optional, later: a short aerial yoga teacher training (weekend or online) for vocabulary, spotting and confidence

## Before every class
- **Rig check:** carabiners locked, swivels/daisy chains not frayed, fabric not torn or snagged. If unsure, take that hammock out of use
- **Height:** usually hip height (low point at the hip crease). Lower for cocoon/savasana if the studio does that
- **Room:** mats under every hammock, enough space between hammocks for full swings
- **Students:** remove rings, watches, belt buckles and zips. Long hair tied. No lotion on arms and legs (it makes the fabric slippery and damages it)
- **Screening (every class, quick and private):** "Anyone new? Any injuries, pregnancy, or anything I should know?"

## Inversions — who should skip them
Studios commonly ask these students to skip inversions (offer a non-inverted option instead):
- Pregnancy
- High or low blood pressure, heart conditions
- Glaucoma, detached retina or other eye conditions; recent eye surgery
- Recent surgery, head injury or concussion
- Vertigo, inner-ear problems
- Acute neck, shoulder or back injury; severe osteoporosis
- Recent Botox or fillers (studios usually say 24h+)
- A heavy meal in the last 1–2 hours, or feeling dizzy or unwell today

If in doubt: "Let's keep you upright today, and check with your doctor before next class."

## During class
- **Demo first, then cue them through it.** Never teach a skill I can't do cleanly and spot
- **Teach the exit first.** Every inversion or flip: show how to come out before going in
- **One inversion group at a time** for new skills. Watch everyone who is upside down
- **Spotting:** stand beside the student, hands ready at the hips/lower back. Don't hold their full weight
- **Time upside down:** short holds for beginners (3–5 breaths), then come up slowly, sit for a breath before standing
- **Dizzy or nauseous:** come down, sit, head level, sip water. No more inversions that class
- **Grip fatigue:** when hands get tired, end grip-heavy drills. Tired hands slip

## My limits
- Advanced drops, rolls and flips: only once I can spot them confidently and the student has the strength first (active hang 20s+, tuck-up with control)
- If a student asks for something I don't know: "Let's build up to that — I'll bring it in a few weeks." Then learn it properly first
