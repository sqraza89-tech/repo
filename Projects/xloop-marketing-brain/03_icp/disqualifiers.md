# Disqualifiers

Naming what xLoop will not pursue is as commercially useful as naming what it will. A firm that
lists nine industries and eleven technologies signals it will take any work; that costs premium
pricing and it costs focus.

**Hard** = do not pursue. **Soft** = pursue only with an explicit reason and an owner.

---

## Hard disqualifiers

| # | Disqualifier | Why |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | **The requirement maps only to an M1/M2 offering** (e.g. Digital Twin, or any partner-owned technology whose ownership is unresolved) | xLoop cannot deliver a reference-grade outcome, and a poor first delivery in a new category is worse than no delivery |
| H2 | **Pure body-shopping on rate card alone** — no technical scope, price is the only criterion | Competes against xLoop's lowest-margin motion and produces no reference value. The ICP research is explicit that the buyer worth having is buying speed and specialism, not headcount |
| H3 | **Client requires a certification xLoop does not hold at corporate level** — e.g. "must be ISO 27001 certified as a company" | The certification is held at **individual** Lead Auditor level. Claiming otherwise is a prohibited claim |
| H4 | **NEPRA Regulation 8 audit where a CERT-recognised Category-1 firm is contractually mandated** | xLoop is not on the PKCERT register. Until resolved, this is a delivery risk, not an opportunity. Partner or reposition instead |
| H5 | **Consumer / B2C AI products** | No evidence of capability or intent anywhere in the corpus |
| H6 | **Work requiring on-site presence in a country with no xLoop entity and no partner** | Four offices: Pakistan, USA, UAE, Qatar |
| H7 | **Prospects requiring published client names xLoop cannot supply** | No grade-A proof exists yet. Better to withdraw than to over-claim |
| H8 | **Anything requiring a claim on the prohibited list** | See `05_brand/prohibited_claims.md` |
| H9 | **Sanctioned entities, or engagements that would breach export controls** | Legal |
| H10 | **Academic/student/jobseeker enquiries treated as commercial leads** | High volume via xCelerate. Route to recruitment; never into pipeline |

## Soft disqualifiers — proceed only with a named reason

| # | Disqualifier | Condition to proceed |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Organisation below the ICP size floor (<500 employees, <$50M revenue) | Strategic logo, a repeatable pattern, or a route into a group |
| S2 | Industry outside Priority 1–3 | A named sponsor and a problem that maps to an M4+ capability |
| S3 | Geography outside Priority/Emerging markets | Inbound with clear budget, or partner-led |
| S4 | Greenfield AI with no data foundation and no appetite to fix it | Only if the engagement starts with a readiness assessment. Otherwise this is the ICP-1 fear coming true, and xLoop will own the blame |
| S5 | Prospect wants a fixed-price AI outcome guarantee | Only with a scoped pilot first |
| S6 | Procurement-led RFP with an incumbent already positioned | Only if there is a genuine relationship or a differentiated requirement (e.g. AI security) |
| S7 | Single-country regulator work outside Pakistan | Only if the regulation has been read and mapped, as NEPRA was |

## Content disqualifiers

Signals that a lead is **not** a buyer, however it looks in analytics:

- Traffic from `agent hospital china`, `cio decision framework e-clinical`, `evaluate the transportation company grab` and similar. `VERIFIED INTERNAL FACT` — these appear in Search Console with meaningful impressions and **zero clicks**. They are research queries, not buying queries, and they are inflating impression counts.
- Job-seeker traffic to careers pages. The GA4 export shows `/careers/jobs` as the **second most-visited page on the entire site**.
- Bootcamp and training traffic. Third most-visited.
- Brand-confusion traffic: `xloop loan app`, `xloop loans`, `x loop loan app` — a different entity. Real users, wrong company.

**Marketing consequence:** at least three of the top five pages by traffic serve non-buyers.
Any traffic target that does not segment buyer traffic from recruitment traffic will report growth
that has no commercial meaning.

---

## How to use this list

1. **In qualification** — Gate 1 fails on any hard disqualifier.
2. **In content briefs** — do not write for a disqualified segment, however large its search volume.
3. **In reporting** — report buyer traffic separately from recruitment and training traffic.
4. **In sales enablement** — H3, H4 and H7 are the three that create real exposure if missed. They belong in the first-call checklist.
