# ICP Master

## 1. The finding that matters

**xLoop already has a better ICP than the one supplied in the Release 1 brief.**

`Documents/Who buys AI transformation services - xLoop ICP Explorations.docx` segments buyers by
**buying motion** rather than by industry or job title, and it opens with the right critique:

> "The biggest mistake I see AI consultancies make is creating ICPs based on job titles
> (CIO, CDO, CTO). The actual segmentation should be based on buying motions."

That is correct, and it is the framework this Marketing Brain adopts. What follows extends it
with evidence from the rest of the corpus, adds a fifth ICP the internal document does not cover
(regulated compliance), and reconciles it against the brief.

**Additional ICPs found in the material:** none that warrant separate treatment. Every industry
appearing in the decks resolves into one of the five below.

## 6. Why this ICP is commercially useful

Each ICP has, and can be tested against:
- a named buying motion (not a title),
- a stated fear that content can address,
- a trigger that can be detected as a signal,
- an entry offer,
- an evidence set the buyer requires before purchase,
- a search cluster.

That makes it operational. A future workflow can take an account, ask "which ICP, which trigger,
which offer, which proof", and produce an answer — which is what Release 2 will need.

## 7. What is still unknown

1. **Which ICPs actually convert.** There is no win/loss record and no CRM. The deal sizes and cycles in the internal document are `SUPPORTED INFERENCE`, not measured history.
2. **Where existing revenue concentrates.** Without that, ICP weighting is a judgement, not a calculation.
3. **Whether xLoop can service ICP 3 at volume** — AI security practice capacity is unknown.
4. **Whether ICP 4 extends beyond Pakistan.** GCC energy regulators have comparable OT security regimes; whether xLoop can serve them is unverified.
