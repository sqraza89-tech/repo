# NEPRA ICP

## 1. The defining fact

`VERIFIED INTERNAL FACT` — research across all 87 obligated companies found:
**3 confirmed CTOs. 0 confirmed CISOs. 68 companies where the CEO was the most senior identifiable
executive. 16 with no identifiable executive at all.**

**This is not a security-buyer market.** The person who must act is a chief executive or plant
director with no security function to delegate to, who has just been told they are subject to an
obligation they did not know existed.

Every marketing decision for this vertical follows from that.

## 3. Buyer map

### Primary buyer — the Executive Sponsor
- **Titles:** CEO, Managing Director, Director, COO, Plant Director.
- **Situation:** legally obligated, no in-house security expertise, possibly already contacted by ISMO.
- **Fear:** a regulator finding, an enforcement action, or a public incident.
- **What they need to hear first:** what the obligation is, whether it applies to them, what happens if they do nothing, and how long the fix takes.
- **What they do not want:** a technical security pitch.

### Technical validator — where one exists
- **Titles:** Head of IT, IT Manager, SCADA/Instrumentation Engineer, Plant Engineering Manager.
- **Their concern is singular and non-negotiable:** *"Will this disrupt plant or SCADA operations?"*
- **The answer must always be present:** the assessment is **non-intrusive by design** — configuration and log review, evidence verification, interviews, and a non-intrusive OT assessment. Controlled penetration testing is confined to the IT perimeter. `VERIFIED INTERNAL FACT` from the ACT Wind proposal methodology.
- The prior work log records that this assurance was nearly edited out of the landing page. **It must not be.**

### Financial approver
- CFO or Finance Director. Concerned with cost, and whether this is one-off or recurring.
- Honest answer: the audit is a project; compliance is recurring (quarterly reporting, annual review, training refresh). Say so — it is both true and commercially better.

### Blocker
- An incumbent IT vendor or systems integrator who would prefer to scope this themselves.
- Counter: xLoop is auditing against a regulation, not selling infrastructure. Independence is the point.

## 5. Messaging rules for this audience

Derived from the audience research and locked in prior work:

1. **Lead executive-legible, put clause detail below.** The reader is a CEO. The first 60 words must say what the obligation is and whether it applies to them.
2. **Never publish the flyer as an image.** Invisible to Google, to AI answer engines and to screen readers. Everything ships as real HTML text.
3. **Do not invent a non-compliance statistic.** This audience would catch a fake number. Use the 72-hour reporting deadline and the ISMO enforcement activity instead — both are real.
4. **Answer the disruption objection early and never let it be edited out.**
5. **Contact is phone and email only** — `tel:+922135869200` and `xsecurity@xloopdigital.com` — matching the mailed flyer. No booking link, no form. Consistency between the letter and the page is itself a trust signal.
6. **The flyer's four service bundles omit Regulation 5 (security controls) and Regulation 7 (data integrity)**, which the audit actually covers. The coverage table closes that gap. **Sales must know the audit is broader than the four headline blocks.**
7. **Certifications are individual, never corporate.** "Certified ISO 27001 and ISO 42001 Lead Auditors" is approved. "xLoop is ISO 27001 certified" is not.
8. **The ACT Wind reference is anonymised** as "a 30 MW wind IPP" — and even that is pending confirmation that it is permitted under the engagement terms.
