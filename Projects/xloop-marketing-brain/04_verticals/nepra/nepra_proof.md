## 3. Prohibited claims for this vertical

❌ **"xLoop is ISO 27001 certified"** or any corporate-level certification claim.
❌ **Naming ACT Wind**, or any detail that identifies them (30 MW *and* Jhimpir *and* two sites together may be identifying — use judgement).
❌ **NIST / IEC 62443 / MITRE ATT&CK alignment** on the website — drafted but **not approved**. Note the tension: the proposal *does* make this claim to clients. Resolve in one direction.
❌ **Any invented non-compliance statistic.** This audience would catch it.
❌ **"CERT-recognised", "Category-1", or any implication of PKCERT registration.** xLoop is not on that register.
❌ **Any claim that xLoop is the required or approved auditor** for Reg. 8.
❌ Publishing the flyer as an image — invisible to search, AI answer engines and screen readers.

## 5. Proof strategy for this vertical

**xLoop cannot win this market on proof.** One delivered audit, anonymised, against competitors
who hold a credential xLoop does not. Competing on track record is a losing argument today.

**xLoop can win it on published expertise.** No competitor has published the requirement. The
market's best-known page has no FAQ at all. The person with the obligation currently cannot find a
clear answer to "what does this actually require of me?" anywhere online.

**Therefore the proof strategy is:**

1. **Be the authoritative published answer to the regulation.** Clause-by-clause, Regs 4–12, plain executive English, in real HTML. This is defensible because it requires reading a gazette rather than buying ads, and it is exactly what AI answer engines will surface.
2. **Substitute methodology for track record.** Publish *how* the audit runs, not *how many* have run. The seven-stage methodology and the non-intrusiveness commitment do more work with this buyer than a client count.
3. **Lead with the credential that is real** — ISO 27001/42001 Lead Auditors — and never stretch it.
4. **Convert the first three engagements into references.** Build client permission into the engagement letter from now on. Three anonymised-but-specific references would change the position materially.
5. **If the Category-1 question resolves badly, partner and say so openly.** "Delivered with [Cat-1 firm], who hold the OT Security CAT-I registration" is a stronger, more honest position than quietly repositioning — and it keeps the 87-company audience.
6. **Seriously evaluate applying to the PKCERT register.** "CERT-recognised" would be the single strongest differentiator available in this market, and xLoop already has the practice to justify an application.
