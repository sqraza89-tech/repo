# Brand Voice

**Source of truth: `xLoop - Brand Guidelines_v1e.pdf` (official).**
Official guidance is quoted below and marked. Where this file goes beyond the guidelines it is
marked as an extension and is a recommendation, not policy.

---

## 1. Official voice attributes

`VERIFIED INTERNAL FACT` — the guidelines define four attributes:

| Attribute | Official definition |
|---|---|
| **Welcoming** | "Each message should make our audience feel part of the xLoop Digital family." |
| **Accessible** | "Our language is straightforward and jargon-free." |
| **Respectful** | "We engage with respect, valuing the time and perspectives of our clients." |
| **Smart** | "Our messages are informed, insightful, and forward-thinking." |

## 2. Official like-this / not-like-this examples

`VERIFIED INTERNAL FACT` — reproduced from the guidelines:

| ✅ Like this | ❌ Not like this |
|---|---|
| "xLoop Digital is here to turn vision into reality. What problems are you facing?" | — |
| "Discover how our technology can streamline your workflow." | "Implement our high-throughput, end-to-end solutions for operational optimization." |
| "We value your insight and look forward to growing together." | "We have the tech know-how you're obviously missing." |
| "Stay ahead by exploring the true potential of AI." | "Utilize our superior tech to avoid falling behind." |

**The pattern is consistent and useful:** the rejected examples are jargon-dense, condescending or
fear-based. The approved ones are plain, generous and invitational.

**⚠️ Compliance problem.** By the guidelines' own standard, a substantial amount of current
material would fail. "Implement our high-throughput, end-to-end solutions for operational
optimization" is listed as **not** xLoop's voice — yet phrases of exactly that register appear
across the current service pages ("comprehensive solutions for every need", "end-to-end solutions
personalized to your needs", "harness the power of AI"). The guidelines are right and the practice
has drifted.

## 3. Brand values

`VERIFIED INTERNAL FACT`: **Innovation · Integrity · Excellence · Collaboration · Agility**

Integrity is the one with operational teeth here: *"We operate with transparency, honesty, and
fairness in all our interactions."* That value is the internal justification for the evidence
discipline in this Marketing Brain. Publishing unsourced statistics is a values problem before it
is a marketing problem.

## 4. Identity language from the guidelines

- **"The 'n+1' consulting mindset"** — "always look beyond the conventional and strive for the extraordinary." A distinctive internal idea, currently unused in market-facing material. *Extension: worth reviving — it is ownable and it is not a cliché.*
- **"Impact-driven Digital Engineering and Consulting"** — the guidelines' own description of the category.
- **Vision:** "to empower global innovation through the principles of Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion."
- **Tagline:** **"Exceeding Expectations"** — *Extension note: this appears on no public surface, and it fails the substitution test. Recommend retiring it or replacing it; see `messaging.md`.*

## 5. Voice by audience — extension

Not in the guidelines. Recommended, because the same voice cannot serve a CEO under regulatory
pressure and a CTO evaluating an architecture.

| Audience | Register | Lead with | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Executive sponsor (ICP 1)** | Confident, plain, commercially literate | Business consequence and risk | Technology names, architecture detail |
| **Data owner (ICP 2)** | Precise, peer-to-peer | The specific failure mode they recognise | Hype, transformation language |
| **Security buyer (ICP 3)** | Direct, technically credible, non-alarmist | The exposure and how it is found | Fear-selling — the guidelines explicitly reject it |
| **Regulated executive (ICP 4)** | Formal, factual, calm | The obligation and whether it applies to them | Sales language, clause detail up front |
| **Engineering leader (ICP 5)** | Concrete, specific, no fluff | Stack, architecture, delivery evidence | Business-value abstraction |

## 6. Sentence-level rules — extension

1. **Lead with the answer.** First 40–60 words state the answer, then explain. This is how people read and how AI answer engines extract.
2. **One idea per sentence.** Average under 22 words.
3. **Name the thing.** "Regulations 4–12", "LangGraph", "ISO 42001" — specificity is the differentiator.
4. **Active voice, present tense.** "We audit" not "audits are conducted".
5. **No superlatives without evidence.** No "leading", "best-in-class", "world-class" unless attributable.
6. **No fear-selling.** Directly contrary to the official voice.
7. **British/US spelling: pick one and hold it.** The corpus mixes "optimise/optimize" and "personalised/personalized" within single documents. `SUPPORTED INFERENCE` — US spelling, given the US HQ and the primary search markets.

## 7. Voice test

Before publishing, four questions:

1. **Substitution** — replace "xLoop" with a competitor's name. Still true? Rewrite.
2. **Jargon** — could a smart non-specialist follow it? If not, it fails "Accessible".
3. **Condescension** — does it imply the reader is behind or ignorant? If so, it fails the guidelines' own examples.
4. **Evidence** — is every number and named capability traceable?
