# Research Rules

How research is conducted, recorded and used across every release.

---

## 1. Before researching

1. **State the question.** A research task without a decision behind it produces material nobody uses.
2. **State what decision it informs**, and who makes that decision.
3. **Check the Marketing Brain first.** If it is already recorded, use it and note the date.
4. **Set the evidence bar.** A public claim needs L1/L2. Internal orientation may use L3.

## 2. During research

1. **Record the source at the moment of capture** — path or URL, date accessed, and for documents the file's modified date.
2. **Quote before paraphrasing.** Capture the exact wording for anything that will become a claim.
3. **Note contradictions rather than resolving them silently.** Every contradiction found in Release 1 is logged, not smoothed over.
4. **Record what you did not read**, and why. Release 1 read ~90 documents in full and inventoried ~250 without reading them — that decision is recorded so a later release can revisit it.
5. **Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence.** "No Microsoft Fabric material was found in the corpus" is not "xLoop has no Fabric capability." It means the marketing evidence does not exist.
6. **Prefer primary sources.** Read the regulation, not a competitor's summary of it. The Reg. 12 discovery came from reading the source advisory letter; the Category-1 claim came from a competitor and remains unverified precisely because of its origin.

## 3. Handling external sources

| Level | Requirements |
|---|---|
| **L2 — External primary** | Name the source and the instrument. For regulations, cite the SRO/gazette reference |
| **L3 — Secondary research** | Name the firm, the report title and the year. Never "studies show" or "research suggests" |
| **L4 — General** | Label as general. Never use as evidence for a claim about xLoop or about a market size |

**Currently unsourced external claims in the corpus** — all requiring a citation before any use:
73% of enterprises suffered an AI-related security breach; $4.8M average incident cost;
64% of GenAI apps have critical vulnerabilities; 82% of financial institutions experienced prompt
injection attempts; WEF estimate of $5.7 trillion by 2030; 71% of telecom leaders reporting AI
vulnerability; 45% of oil and gas cyberattacks targeting AI systems.

These may well be accurate. **Uncited, they are unusable** — and in an AI security pitch to a CISO,
an uncited statistic is the fastest way to lose credibility with the one buyer who will check.

## 4. Competitive research

1. **Treat competitor claims as claims, not facts.** The Category-1 assertion came from a firm with a commercial incentive to make it. It was checked against the PKCERT register directly, and the check found the register does not mention NEPRA at all.
2. **Verify against primary sources.**
3. **Record the incentive.** Who benefits from this being true?
4. **Never publish a characterisation of a named competitor.** Internal analysis only.
5. **Do not scrape.** Read what is public, manually, in Release 1.

## 5. Market sizing

1. Distinguish TAM, SAM and SOM explicitly.
2. Record the derivation, not just the number.
3. **Never publish internal market sizing.** The xCrowdIQ Qatar analysis is confidential.
4. Treat vendor-published market sizes as L4 unless the underlying methodology is available.

## 6. Search research

`VERIFIED INTERNAL FACT` — **xLoop has no keyword volume tool.** Every "search demand" figure in the
corpus is Google Trends *relative interest*, which is not volume and cannot be compared across terms.

Therefore:
1. **Never state a search volume** unless it comes from a tool that measures volume.
2. Google Trends shows relative interest over time for a single term. Say so.
3. Search Console shows real impressions and clicks **for pages that already rank** — it is evidence about existing performance, not about market demand.
4. **Prioritise by commercial intent, not by apparent volume.** "nepra compliance audit" is worth more than "ai consulting" at a fraction of the volume.

## 7. Recording

Every research output records: the question · what was found · sources with dates · evidence classes ·
contradictions · gaps (`EVIDENCE REQUIRED`) · the recommendation · what would change it.

**That last field matters most.** A recommendation that cannot be falsified cannot be improved.

## 8. Re-verification

| Item | Cadence |
|---|---|
| Live website positioning | Quarterly |
| Corporate facts | On change, and quarterly |
| Approved claims | Quarterly |
| Competitor landscape | Half-yearly, or on a trigger |
| Regulatory position (NEPRA) | **Monthly** — it is live and enforcing |
| Search performance | Monthly |
| ICP and personas | Annually, or when win/loss data first becomes available |
